## 6.1.0

Screen kinds and the pause-menu look. Every element now draws for the kind of screen it is on: MenuKit's grey look on container screens (inventories, chests), vanilla's pause-menu look on options screens (Options, Video Settings, Controls, Create World). A settings page can let the player pick items with the new item picker. A column of buttons can wrap into more columns where the screen's frame ends, and a panel can pop up over every other panel on the screen.

### Added

- `ScreenKind` (`CONTAINER`, `OPTIONS`) and one rule for where it comes from, `ScreenKind.of(screen)`: a vanilla `AbstractContainerScreen` is a container screen, any other vanilla screen an options screen, a standalone `MKScreen` the kind it declares when it is made (`super(title, ScreenKind.OPTIONS, panels)`, read back with `screenKind()`), and one that declares none a container screen, as before. Slot-group panels and the HUD are container. A dialog or any panel on a screen is that screen's kind. The kind travels in `RenderContext.kind()`, so every element follows it, inside sections, scroll areas, tabs and rows, and inside elements built from other elements (Keybindery's key button).
- Text colour by role. `TextRole` (`HEADING`, `BODY`, `MUTED`, `DISABLED`), `TextLabel.Builder.role(...)`, and `ScreenKind.textColor(role)` and `textShadow()`: dark grey on a container screen, vanilla's white and greys with a shadow on an options screen. A label with no role is `BODY`, which on a container screen is the colour it was. Checkbox, radio and section header labels follow the same table. `ElementConstants.TEXT_MUTED` names MenuKit's muted grey.
- On an options screen, the controls in vanilla's sprites: `Button` and the dropdowns are vanilla's buttons, `Checkbox` vanilla's checkbox, `Toggle` a vanilla button whose text is its state (its label, or vanilla's ON and OFF), a `Section` header in the Controls screen's category colours (read from the left, in line with its content, not centred as vanilla's is), a `Divider` vanilla's separator. A panel's `RAISED` background is vanilla's popup frame, so a dialog reads as vanilla's own.
- `ScrollContainer` on an options screen is the band every Options screen has: vanilla's darker list background, a separator above and below, and vanilla's thin list scroller. Its content is inset a little wider than vanilla's lists: `ScrollContainer.OPTIONS_INSET_X` (14) from the left, the scrollbar lane on the right, `OPTIONS_INSET_Y` (10) above the first child and below the last, scrolling with the content. Size content for it with the new `ScrollContainer.viewportWidthFor(width, kind)`. A panel that auto-scrolls on an options screen lays its elements out inside the inset itself.
- `Tabs` in `WRAP` and `SIDE_SCROLL` on an options screen is the tab bar of Create World: white labels, the selected one underlined and open into a page of the list background, the separator on either side. The page insets its body the same way, `OPTIONS_INSET_X` from both sides (the scrollbar lane on the right when it scrolls) and `OPTIONS_INSET_Y` above and below, so a spacer row, a full-width divider and wrapped text stay inside the margin. `SIDEBAR` on an options screen is that tab bar turned on its side: vanilla's tab sprites turned a quarter, open toward the body on the right, the selected one open into a page of the list background beside the column, vanilla's separator down the column's edge above and below the tabs, labels upright and white with the selected one underlined. Its page insets the body as the top strip's does. On a container screen it keeps MenuKit's drawing.
- `InputContext.kind()`, the kind of screen, as `RenderContext.kind()` carries it, so an element answers input with the geometry it drew. The five-argument constructor stays and means a container screen.
- `Tabs.Builder.bodyScroll(Function<String, Double> get, BiConsumer<String, Double> set)`: each tab's body scroll as consumer state, in pixels from the top keyed by tab id, as `ScrollContainer.state` holds a container's. A screen rebuilt on the same lens opens each body where it was on the first frame, with no slide from the top. Without it the scroll is the element's own, as before.
- `MKTooltip.avoidCoverWhen(BooleanSupplier)`: while it holds, a tooltip goes on the side of the cursor where it covers less of the screen (a container screen's frame with an open recipe book, shown MenuKit panels, the screen's widgets), its text wrapped to the room on that side. A side is ruled out only when the tooltip cannot wrap to fit it: narrower than its longest word, an image in it, or `MKTooltip.MIN_WRAP_WIDTH` (60). Otherwise the side that covers less wins, however narrow; a tie goes to vanilla's side, and no side left places it as vanilla does. Off unless a mod registers it, like `wrapWhen` and `clampWhen`.
- `ItemPicker`, a setting whose value is an item: a slot with a lens onto an `Item` (`state(get, set)`, `Items.AIR` for none). A click opens a chooser over the screen, of the same kind: every item in a scrolling grid, a search field, Clear and Cancel. A right click empties it. It is client-only and never touches the inventory or the server: the item is a setting value, not the player's. It draws as vanilla's slot on an options screen and MenuKit's on a container screen.

The kind changes how an element draws, never its size or behaviour, except that the options list band (a `ScrollContainer`, a `Tabs` page) insets its content. `Slider` and `TextField` are vanilla's own widgets and look the same on both. Where vanilla has no twin (radio buttons, info boxes, progress bars, colour swatches, consumer sprites) MenuKit's drawing stays.

- `Flow.builder().vertical()`: a column that fills top to bottom and wraps into a new column to the right. It wraps at the frame band, the room from its top down to the bottom of the screen's frame (`topPos + imageHeight` on a container screen), which a panel in a side region (`LEFT_ALIGN_*`, `RIGHT_ALIGN_*`) gets from its host on a container screen, on a slot group (the band runs to the container screen's frame, not the group's box) and beside a standalone screen's main panel. The panel widens with each column, so a `RIGHT_*` region still places it beside the frame. A hidden child takes no room, so the column reflows when one hides. In a column `gap(horizontal, vertical)` is between columns and between children. Elsewhere the Flow is one column. The band reaches elements through the new `PanelElement.wrapHeight(int)` (default no-op); it is not a scroll ceiling, so nothing else about a side panel's size changes.
- `Panel.Builder.popover()` and `Panel.isPopover()`: a panel in the new POPOVER layer, between the ordinary (FLOW) panels and the modal OVERLAY. A popover draws above every ordinary panel of every host on the screen, whichever registered first and whichever host holds either (an adapter's popover over a slot-group panel), and below a modal and its dim. Claims and pointer input follow the same order, so a click, the wheel, hover or a tooltip over a popover never reach a panel drawn under it. On a container screen popovers draw after the ordinary panels and before the created slots, so a popover's own slots draw on it; another panel's created slot under a popover still draws over it.

### Changed

- One draw order, across hosts, decides both what draws on top and what takes the pointer: layer by layer (FLOW, POPOVER, OVERLAY), host by host, then each host's sorted order. Claims already read it that way. Element dispatch for a point no panel claims (a see-through panel's controls) now does too: it walked each host's panels in turn, upper host first, whatever their layer, so an upper host's ordinary panel was offered a click before a lower host's centred overlay.
- §0065 amendment candidate: it says z-order is sorted order. Within a layer it still is; across layers the order is FLOW below POPOVER below OVERLAY, which overlays already did without being written down. The record could say "z-order is the layer plan's order: layers bottom to top, hosts bottom to top within a layer, sorted order within a host".
- Panels injected into a vanilla screen that is not a container (the pause screen, Options, Controls, a mod's config screen) draw in vanilla's look there, where they drew MenuKit's grey.
- `Tabs` strips scroll the way a `ScrollContainer` does, both on one scroller: by pixels, 10 a wheel notch, where the wheel used to move the sidebar and the side-scroll row a whole tab. An arrow, or a selected tab partly off the strip, slides it there instead of jumping.
- The `SIDEBAR`'s scrollbar is a `ScrollContainer`'s: the same fixed 15 px handle and track, in the same look per kind of screen (MenuKit's inset track on a container screen, vanilla's thin list scroller on an options screen). Its handle was sized to the share of the column in view, with a 32 px floor, so a column a tab or two too tall had a handle nearly the track's length. On a container screen its lane is the wider track's.
- Pressing a `ScrollContainer`'s track, off the handle, jumps the handle there and holds it, as the sidebar's track did and vanilla's lists and creative inventory do. It used to do nothing.
- `ScrollContainer` with no `contentHeight` reads its children's extent every frame, so the range follows a child that grows or shrinks while shown.

### Deprecated

- `Tabs.SIDEBAR_SCROLLBAR_WIDTH`. The sidebar's scrollbar is as wide as a `ScrollContainer`'s on that kind of screen (`ScrollContainer.TRACK_WIDTH` on a container screen, 6 on an options screen). It goes in 7.0.0.
- `ControlStyle`, and `style(ControlStyle)` on `Button`, `Dropdown` and `DropdownMulti`. They do nothing now, since nothing picks a look but the kind of screen, and they go in 7.0.0. A mod built against 6.0.0 still loads. Delete the call; on an options screen the element already draws in vanilla's look.

### Fixed

- A `Tabs` body or a panel that scrolled, then fitted (a section closed), then scrolled again (the section reopened) jumped back to its old offset instead of starting from the top. A body that fits now sets its offset to the top.
- Input to a `Tabs` body before its first draw (a screen just rebuilt) went nowhere; the body is laid out for input as for drawing.
- A dialog or a panel laid out with `Row` or `Column` can be built at mod init, where adapters are declared. `Row` and `Column` measured their elements in `build()`, and a label's or button's size needs the game's font, which does not exist yet at init, so `ConfirmDialog` and `AlertDialog` could only be built after the declaration freeze had closed. They now place their elements on the first read of any element's position, in practice the panel's first layout pass. A `Row` or `Column` holding a custom `ElementSpec` still lays out in `build()`, and a spacer without `width(px)` still throws there.
